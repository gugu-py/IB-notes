**1.How does Matika Wilbur's use of photography in Project 562 challenge common stereotypes and misrepresentations of Native American communities?**

The common stereotypes and misrepresentations of Native American communities are:
- the community is fading
- Native Americans were savages
- Native Americans were drunks, etc.

In Project 562, Matika Wilbur's goal was to 'unveil the true essence of contemporary Native issues, the beauty of Native culture, the magnitude of tradition, and expose her vitality.' By taking photos of these Native Americans, the author was able to show the true aspect of the Native Americans to the world, so that people are able to have a better understanding of their identity, their culture, their current situation and their future.



**2.Analyze how Wilbur's artistic choices, such as her use of color, framing, and inclusion of both traditional and modern elements, contribute to the overall meaning and impact of her work.**

To meet this goal, she applied several devices, such as composition, lighting, specific subject seletion, etc.

By using certain color pallete, the author was able to transmit certain feeling to audience. For example, in the picture of a grandmother and her grand daughter, the pinkish gray and the orange of the dawn give the audience a sense of hope and peace. In addition, the vibrant traditional clothing stands out against the hued natural surroundings, emphasizing the unique identity of their culture.

By using framing, Wilbur was able to emphasize some elements in the picture to show the theme more clearly. Again take the picture of a grandmother and her grand daughter as example. The expansive framing places the subjects within a vast natural landscape, connecting them to their cultural roots and origin. The balanced composition(placing half human half nature) with horizontal lines create a metaphor for continuity between human experience and the environment, and thus reflect harmony between human and nature.

Wilbur also carefully selected subject in her pictures to reveal some deeper details and stories. In the picture of a grandmother and her grand daughter, the subjects’ facial expressions and posture show wisdom and quiet strength. Two individual were all gazing at something that is far away. These highlight the passing down of knowledge, cultural values, and hope for the future. Furthermore, the elder was holding the child's hand. This symbolizes the protective and nurturing role of the elder in passing down traditions.

**3.Discuss the significance of the themes of resilience and adaptability in Wilbur's photographs. How do these themes contribute to a more nuanced understanding of Native American identity and experience?**

The themes of resilience and adaptability in Wilbur's photographs provide a unique perspective of Native American identity. By selecting subjects in natural landscapes that symbolize their deep connection to land, Wilbur underscores a strength rooted in cultural continuity despite historical adversity. For instance, the elder holding a child’s hand conveys resilience through generational ties, showing that, while adapting to modern challenges, the community continues passing tradition to the next generation.

**4.Evaluate the effectiveness of Wilbur's approach in using photography as a medium to share the stories and perspectives of diverse Native American individuals and communities. Consider the potential impact of her work on the viewer's understanding and appreciation of Indigenous cultures.**

Compared to other medium to share the stories and perspectives, photography has these advantages that make Wilbur effective:
1. **Visual Impact and Emotional Connection**: Photography captures real-life moments, often creating an immediate emotional response in viewers. A single image can convey complex emotions, sparking empathy or awareness about the subject matter. Wilbur’s images, such as a grandmother and granddaughter in a vast landscape, immediately connect viewers to Indigenous spirituality and tradition. This visual storytelling evokes empathy, enhancing viewers understanding on cultural roots. For example, the picture of grandmother and her grand daughter created a strong sense of peace and hope.
2. **Authenticity and Realism**: Unlike other mediums, photography is often seen as a reflection of reality, making it a trusted medium for storytelling. The photographic details—like the elder’s wrinkles and gray hair—add depth, showcasing age, wisdom, and resilience, which written mediums might struggle to convey as naturally.
3. **Accessibility and Universal Language**: Photographs transcend language barriers, allowing people from diverse backgrounds to connect with the same image. This universal appeal makes it an accessible medium for communicating stories and perspectives. The subjects’ vibrant traditional clothing and the warm dawn colors resonate globally, making Indigenous values and identities accessible to diverse audiences.
4. **Symbolic Depth**: Wilbur uses visual metaphors, like dawn representing renewal and an elder holding a child’s hand symbolizing cultural continuity, to deepen viewers’ understanding of Native resilience.

Wilbur’s photography effectively captures and shares Native American stories by leveraging the unique strengths of the medium. Wilbur’s photography fosters appreciation and understanding of Indigenous cultures, making it an impactful medium for storytelling.
