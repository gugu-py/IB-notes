# Consequences of the Indian Revolution in 1857

## Direct Control by Great Britain
- Because of the revolution, the British government decided to abolish the power of the East India Company and take over the rule of India directly.
- In 1858, the "Government of India Act" declared that India was a royal colony of Britain.

## Changes in Political Structure
- The British government became more centralized as they assumed full control over India.
- To prevent future revolutions, Britain monitored India more closely and intensely, especially at the local level, weakening traditional aristocracy and local leaders while empowering the British local army.

## Socio-Cultural Changes
- The failed revolts increased the cultural hegemony imposed by the British on the Indian people.
- Since many of the Indian soldiers were Muslims and Hindus, the British strengthened their divide and rule system to prevent a united revolution by Indians.

## Physical Impact on Local People
- Following the revolt's failure, fear and animosity towards the British Empire grew among Indians. Many believed that internal disagreements and a lack of organization would prevent any future success against Britain.
- However, the revolution of 1857 laid the groundwork for future independence movements in India by encouraging nationalistic sentiments.
